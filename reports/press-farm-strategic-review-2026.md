# Press Farm — Strategic & Financial Review
### Mid-Year 2026, with Outlook to 2028

*Prepared June 11, 2026 · Source of truth: `deliveries` + `delivery_items` (Press Farm OS production database) · All figures are realized delivery revenue unless noted*

---

## Executive Summary

Press Farm has moved from a side-of-the-plate herb supplier into a **structurally profitable, fast-growing specialty produce operation** with one anchor customer. Three things define the situation:

1. **Growth is real and accelerating.** Realized revenue went from **$34.2K** (half-year 2024) to **$102.5K** (full-year 2025). The first five months of 2026 already delivered **$42.3K — up 103% on the same period last year.** This is not seasonal noise; it is a step-change in volume and price realization.

2. **The economics are unusually clean.** Across 24 months the farm booked **~$180.8K of revenue against ~$34.4K of cash inputs — a 19% input-cost ratio.** The constraint on the business is not cost; it is throughput and channel.

3. **The risk is concentration, not demand.** **96% of revenue comes from a single restaurant (Press), and 13% of all-time revenue comes from a single crop (Nasturtium).** The franchise is healthy but fragile on two axes — one customer, one hero SKU.

**The "so what":** 2026 is on track to close at **~$120–125K (base case), roughly +20% on 2025.** The upside to a **$145K+** year and a **$200K+ 2027** is available — but it is gated almost entirely by *channel diversification*, not by the farm's ability to grow product. The single highest-value move over the next 18 months is converting the second and third restaurant accounts from a rounding error into 25–30% of revenue.

## 1. Situation Assessment — Where the Business Stands

### 1.1 The growth curve

| Period | Revenue | Deliveries | Avg / delivery |

| 2025 (full year) | $102,501 | 209 | $490 |

The story is in two numbers: **deliveries roughly held flat year-over-year, but revenue per delivery rose ~65% from 2024 to 2026.** Growth is coming from *basket value and price* — more premium SKUs (flowers, microgreens, specialty veg) per drop — not from running the truck more often. That is the high-margin way to grow.

### 1.2 Like-for-like proof point

Stripping out seasonality by comparing identical calendar windows:

|---|---:|---:|---:|

A doubling on a clean like-for-like basis is the single most important fact in this review. Whatever changed between early 2025 and early 2026 — catalog depth, pricing discipline, the move off Excel onto the ordering system — **it worked, and it should be protected and pressed.**

## 2. Revenue Anatomy — What's Actually Driving the Number

### 2.1 Customer concentration (the central risk)

| Restaurant | Revenue (all-time) | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **Press** | $174,271 | **96.4%** |
| Under-Study | $5,778 | 3.2% |
| Events | $756 | 0.4% |

This is the headline risk. The business is, financially, **a single-account supplier.** Press is clearly a strong, growing, loyal account — but the loss, relocation, or chef-turnover of that one relationship would erase the franchise overnight. Under-Study exists in the system but has never been activated as a real channel.

**Top SKUs:** Nasturtium **$23,751 (13% of the entire business)**, Carrots $17,138, Radish $9,745, Mustard Flowers $7,650, Squash $6,803, Basil $6,732, Tomatoes $6,198, Figs $5,312. The **top three crops = 28% of all revenue.**

Two reads:

- **Fragility:** a Nasturtium crop failure, a pest year, or a chef who rotates off the garnish is a 13% revenue event. Microgreens — the highest-controllability, year-round, weather-independent line — is **only 5% of revenue despite a dedicated production module.**

## 3. Outlook — Rest of 2026

We are at the start of the strong half. H1 2026 has banked **$44.1K**; the Aug–Sep peak is still ahead. Three scenarios for **full-year 2026**, anchored on the 2025 H2 actual of $75.5K and the demonstrated +100% YoY momentum:

| Scenario | H2 2026 assumption | FY 2026 | vs 2025 |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Bear** | H2 softens to ~$62K (peak underperforms, weather) | **~$106K** | +4% |
| **Base** | H2 ≈ 2025 + modest growth, ~$78K | **~$122K** | **+19%** |
| **Bull** | 2026 momentum carries, H2 ~$100K | **~$144K** | +41% |

**Base case: ~$122K, +19%.** The bull case is genuinely reachable *if and only if* the Aug–Sep peak is supplied to its full demand — meaning the binding constraint this summer is **production capacity and harvest labor, not orders.** Plan crew and succession plantings now for the peak you can already see coming.

**Near-term watch items (next 90 days):**
- **June is running light** ($1.8K through the 11th). Normal shoulder, but confirm July succession plantings are in to hit the Aug ramp.
- **Protect the Nasturtium and flower lines** — they are 35% of revenue between them and the peak season is their season. A failure here is the single biggest miss available.
- **Activate Under-Study for real** before peak. Even a few hundred dollars a week from a second account compounds into both revenue *and* de-risking.

## 4. Outlook — 2027 and Beyond

### 4.1 The base trajectory

If the farm simply holds its current trajectory — same accounts, same catalog, continued price/basket maturation — **2027 lands around $135–155K** and growth naturally decelerates toward a single-account ceiling. You can only sell so much to one kitchen.

### 4.2 The three levers that change the slope

The difference between a **$150K plateau and a $250K+ business** is not farming harder. It is three structural moves, in priority order:

**Lever 1 — Channel diversification (highest value).**
Moving from 1 active account to 3–4 is the unlock. The system is *already built* for it — multi-restaurant ordering, magic-link chef accounts, Events pseudo-restaurant, offer sheets. If Under-Study + two new Napa Valley kitchens reach even half of Press's run-rate, that is **+$60–80K/year** and it cuts single-account risk from 96% to ~50%. **This is the #1 recommendation of this review.**

**Lever 2 — Fill the winter trough.**
Dec–Mar averages ~$4K/month against an Aug–Sep ~$13–14K. Microgreens (weather-independent, already a production module at only 5% of revenue), plus value-added/preserved product, can realistically add **$3–5K/month of off-season revenue** — smoothing cash flow and improving the farm's negotiating posture as a *year-round* supplier rather than a seasonal one.

**Lever 3 — Mix shift toward flowers & micros.**
Flowers already throw off $40K at the highest revenue-per-unit in the catalog (5,085 units → $40K vs herbs' 84,023 units → $69K). Edible flowers and microgreens are where a small acreage produces the most dollars per square foot and the most defensible chef pull. Tilting the plan another 5–10 points toward these categories raises revenue *without* needing more land.

### 4.3 Illustrative 2027–2028 path

| | 2026 (base) | 2027 (target) | 2028 (target) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Active restaurant accounts | 1.x | 3 | 4–5 |
| Revenue | ~$122K | **~$185K** | **~$260K** |
| Single-account share | ~95% | ~60% | ~45% |
| Winter-month floor | ~$4K | ~$7K | ~$9K |

These targets are deliberately tied to the *account count and mix*, not to a growth percentage — because that is the actual driver. Hit the account math and the revenue follows.

## 6. Risks & Watch-Outs

- **Single-customer dependency (high / structural):** 96% in one account. Mitigation = Imperative 1. This is the risk that matters.
- **Single-crop dependency (medium):** Nasturtium at 13%. Diversify hero SKUs.
- **Key-person / chef turnover (medium):** Press uses rotating chef staff; the *relationship* is institutional but the *orderer* is not. The ordering system + order accountability (`order_audit`) already help here — keep it.
- **Weather / biological (medium, seasonal):** Concentrated in the Aug–Nov window. Microgreens and protected production are the hedge.
- **Measurement blind spot (low, fixable):** Labor uncaptured → net margin unknown. Cheap to fix, valuable to have before any pricing or hiring decision.
