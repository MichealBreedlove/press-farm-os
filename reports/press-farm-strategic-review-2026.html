Executive Summary

Press Farm has moved from a side-of-the-plate herb supplier into a structurally profitable, fast-growing specialty produce operation with one anchor customer. Three findings define the situation:

1

Growth is real and accelerating. Realized revenue rose from $34.2K (half-year 2024) to $102.5K (full-year 2025). The first five months of 2026 delivered $42.3K — up 103% on the same period last year. This is not seasonal noise; it is a step-change in volume and price realization.

2

The economics are unusually clean. Across 24 months the farm booked ~$180.8K of revenue against ~$34.4K of cash inputs — a 19% input-cost ratio, of which nearly half is one-time equipment buildout. The constraint on the business is not cost; it is throughput and channel.

3

The risk is concentration, not demand. 96% of revenue comes from a single restaurant (Press), and 13% of all-time revenue comes from a single crop (Nasturtium). The franchise is healthy but fragile on two axes — one customer, one hero SKU.

The "So What"

2026 is on track to close at ~$120–125K (base case), roughly +20% on 2025. The upside to a $145K+ year and a $200K+ 2027 is available — but it is gated almost entirely by channel diversification, not by the farm's ability to grow product. The single highest-value move over the next 18 months is converting the second and third restaurant accounts from a rounding error into 25–30% of revenue.

04Outlook — 2027 and Beyond

4.1  The base trajectory

If the farm simply holds its current trajectory — same accounts, same catalog, continued price and basket maturation — 2027 lands around $135–155K and growth naturally decelerates toward a single-account ceiling. You can only sell so much to one kitchen.

4.2  The three levers that change the slope

The difference between a $150K plateau and a $250K+ business is not farming harder. It is three structural moves, in priority order:

Lever 1 — Channel diversification (highest value). Moving from one active account to 3–4 is the unlock. The system is already built for it — multi-restaurant ordering, magic-link chef accounts, the Events channel, offer sheets. If Under-Study plus two new Napa Valley kitchens reach even half of Press's run-rate, that is +$60–80K per year and cuts single-account risk from 96% to ~50%. This is the #1 recommendation of this review.

Lever 2 — Fill the winter trough. Dec–Mar averages ~$4K/month against an Aug–Sep ~$13–14K. Microgreens (weather-independent, already a built production module at only 5% of revenue) plus value-added/preserved product can realistically add $3–5K/month of off-season revenue — smoothing cash flow and repositioning the farm as a year-round supplier rather than a seasonal one.

Lever 3 — Mix shift toward flowers & micros. Flowers already throw off $40K at the highest revenue-per-unit in the catalog (5,085 units → $40K, versus herbs' 84,023 units → $69K). Edible flowers and microgreens are where small acreage produces the most dollars per square foot and the most defensible chef pull. Tilting the crop plan another 5–10 points toward these categories raises revenue without more land.
