**Prepared for Chef Phil · Fall Planting Meeting · June 2026**
*Press Farm, Yountville — Micheal Breedlove*

Everything below is built from what the farm grows now or can seed this fall. Two goals: ferments that earn a place on the menu, and jars that look good enough to live on a shelf in the dining room.

## 3. Decoration Jars — Built to Be Looked At

Ranked by how long they hold their looks on a shelf:

1. **Pink turnip torshi** — entire jar glows pink; holds color for months
2. **Red cabbage kraut** — vivid magenta, dense pack, classic crock look
3. **Beet ferments / kvass** — deepest ruby in the lineup
4. **Watermelon radish rounds** — pink ring pattern pressed against the glass
5. **Giardiniera** — multi-color confetti (colored cauliflower earns its keep here)
6. **Whole cherry tomatoes** — jewel tones suspended in clear brine
7. **Dilly beans** — packed vertical with dill fronds; clean, architectural
8. **Chioggia beet slices** — candy-stripe spiral against the glass
9. **Hot pepper mash** — small jars, deep red-orange
10. **Shiso-dyed jars** — anything brined with red shiso goes magenta

**Display notes:**
- Beet, red cabbage, turmeric, and chile ferments hold color longest. Pale ferments (green kraut, fennel) go grey under direct light — keep those in the walk-in, show the colorful ones.
- 2–3% salt brine + a glass weight keeps brine clear and everything submerged; surface exposure is what clouds a jar.
- Active display jars need burping or airlock lids. For permanent decor, ferment fully, then refrigerate or display sealed past active fermentation.

| When | Action |
